Sprint Drag Carry Technique for a Better AFT Event Score

The sprint drag carry is a short event that punishes poor technique and wasted energy. It combines a sprint, a sled drag, lateral shuffles, a kettlebell carry and a final sprint. Smart technique can save seconds without extra fitness.

Start with the sprint. Drive low for the first few steps with a forward lean, then rise gradually. Short, quick steps beat long reaching strides. Keep your arms bent and punching forward and back, not across your body.

For the sled drag, stay low. Lean back slightly with the straps tight, sink your hips, and take powerful short steps while keeping the sled moving. Stopping and restarting costs more energy than a steady pull. Switch your grip hand placement only if the rules allow and only when necessary.

The lateral shuffle needs a wide athletic stance. Push off the trailing foot, keep your toes pointed forward, and never cross your feet. Touch the line with your hand, stay low, and change direction quickly. Practice this with cones and a timer to find the rhythm.

For the kettlebell carry, keep your ribs stacked over your hips, shoulders back and down, and grip firm. Walk fast but under control. Breathe steadily. A swinging bell slows you down, so keep the weights close to your body.

The last sprint is where you empty the tank. Many people save too little for it. Practice finishing the whole event on tired legs by running the sequence twice with five minutes of rest.

Train it twice per week. One session focuses on technique at sixty to seventy percent effort, and one on timed full efforts. Add heavy sled pushes and pulls, carries and short sprints to build strength. Record your times each week.

Keep notes on surface and weather too, since grass, turf and indoor floors feel very different.

A sample week might look like this. Monday: technique session with four easy rounds of the full sequence, focusing on smooth transitions. Wednesday: heavy sled work, six pulls of twenty meters and four farmer carries of forty meters. Friday: two timed full efforts with five minutes of rest between them. Keep the other days for running, deadlifts and easy mobility so you arrive fresh.

Equipment is simple. A sled or a towel on a smooth floor, two kettlebells or dumbbells, and cones will cover most practice. If you have no sled, push a loaded cart or drag a weighted tire with a strap. Wear the shoes you will use on test day so your grip on the surface feels familiar.

Finally, watch your pacing across the whole event. Treat the first sprint as a controlled acceleration, not a full effort, because the drag will tax your legs. Aim for smooth, even speed through the middle and a hard finish. Soldiers who learn this rhythm often take several seconds off without any extra fitness.
